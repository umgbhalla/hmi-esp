## Design Context

### Users
The primary user operates a small always-on ESP32-S3 reflective display at a
glance and occasionally uses its physical controls. The main job is to see
local time, connectivity, environment, recording activity, and system health
without reading a dense diagnostic console. Deep telemetry remains available
for debugging on secondary pages.

### Brand Personality
Precise, calm, capable. The device should feel like a purpose-built instrument,
not a generic dashboard template or a terminal squeezed onto a display.

### Aesthetic Direction
Flagship-quality 1-bit monochrome. Use solid black and white pixels only: no
checkerboards, fake gray fills, gradients, or decorative card grids. Static,
screen-anchored spatial halftone is allowed sparingly for clock depth and
chamfered corners; it must never animate or compete with the heavier active
digits. Prefer bold numerals, strong silhouettes, graphical meters, sparklines,
icons, and deliberate negative space. The 300x400 portrait panel must remain
readable at arm's length.

### Design Principles
1. Put current local time, date, and overall system state at the top level.
2. Show changing values graphically before exposing raw diagnostic text.
3. Keep the home screen glanceable; progressively disclose detailed pipelines.
4. Make BOOT and KEY behavior visible and reversible; never freeze repainting.
5. Respect the physical panel: crisp 1-bit geometry and deterministic patterns.
